% ===== Preamble ============================================================
Professor Guha,

The validation programme is complete --- and more complete than the version scoped in July. Beyond
the five run groups originally planned, we executed the full experimental matrix on \textbf{four
models across three families} (Qwen2.5-7B, Qwen2.5-1.5B, Llama-3.1-8B, Mistral-7B): five-seed
replication, ten-cycle continual learning, the warm-up extension, and all baselines, identically on
each model. Total compute across the programme was roughly 25 GPU-hours ($\approx$USD~\$20). This
report gives the numbers, states plainly which claims survived and which did not, and closes with
what we believe the paper now contributes.

Three results frame everything. First, the paper's central findings are now \textbf{universal across
every model we can test at this scale}: the free-form recall floor sits at $\mathrm{DRA} \approx
0.006$ on all four models while the same models follow in-context facts at up to $0.99$, and internal
proxy validation falsely confirms $93$--$100\%$ of consolidations everywhere. Second, our own
multi-cycle claim (C6) failed replication in an instructive way: across nine seeded ten-cycle
run-pairs, the safety machinery's preservation plateau is universal in \emph{shape} but varies by
three orders of magnitude in \emph{level} across seeds --- so the comparison we previously reported
is not wrong so much as unstable, and the honest finding is that fixed-horizon single-run
comparisons in this field are unreliable. Third, the proposed warm-up repair is a replicated null:
sixteen runs, four models, recall never moved.

Every architecture passed a ground-truth verification gate before any experiment ran: three checks
asserting that KV injection is bit-identical with an empty bank, effective with a populated one, and
exactly reversible when disabled. The gate rejected two defective configurations during the
programme --- one config-encoding fault and one genuine Qwen-specific assumption in the attention
patch --- both fixed before a single invalid number was produced. No model failed the repaired gate;
Llama's identity check passed at $\mathrm{max}|\mathrm{diff}| = 0$.

% ===== 2. Universal findings ===============================================
\section{Findings that are now universal}

\subsection{The recognition--recall floor}

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}l c c c c@{}}
\toprule
 & Qwen-7B & Qwen-1.5B & Llama-8B & Mistral-7B \\
\midrule
Parametric recall (DRA, 5 seeds) & $0.006$ & $0.007$ & $0.006$ & $0.007$ \\
In-context recall (same facts)   & $0.760$ & $0.700$ & $0.987$ & $0.927$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Every model recalls the facts nearly perfectly when they are visible in context, and at floor when
they are stored parametrically through the consolidation pipeline. The dissociation is a property of
the mechanism, not of any one model's pretraining. The weaker-prior hypothesis --- that a smaller
model would let memory steer generation more easily --- is falsified at 1.5B.

\subsection{The self-grading discrepancy}

Across the four models, the internal surprise-reduction proxy certifies between $4$ and $39$
consolidations per run (families differ greatly in how many candidates their surprise statistics
allocate), yet at most a fraction of one per run survives a direct recall test: false-confirmation
rates of $93$--$100\%$ everywhere. What began as your review item \#6 --- ``add a direct gate'' ---
has become, in our view, one of the paper's two most durable contributions: proxy self-validation in
continual learning can be near-totally wrong, invariantly across architectures.

% ===== 3. The correction ===================================================
\section{The correction: long-horizon comparisons are unstable}

The interim position (single seed, ten cycles) was that naive fine-tuning overtakes SLEEP by cycle
ten. Nine seeded run-pairs across the four models show that position was itself under-sampled:

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}l l c c c c@{}}
\toprule
Model & Seed & SLEEP BCP@10 & Naive BCP@10 & SLEEP DRA & Naive DRA \\
\midrule
Qwen-7B    & 0 & 4.53   & 3.36  & 0.050 & 0.085 \\
Qwen-7B    & 1 & 10.20  & 6.42  & 0.032 & 0.080 \\
Qwen-7B    & 2 & 2.71   & 5.44  & 0.057 & 0.078 \\
Qwen-1.5B  & 0 & 15.96  & 21.01 & 0.045 & 0.035 \\
Qwen-1.5B  & 1 & 230.1  & 45.83 & 0.012 & 0.048 \\
Llama-8B   & 0 & 4.83   & 6.09  & 0.032 & 0.083 \\
Llama-8B   & 1 & 1530.9 & 7.11  & 0.045 & 0.075 \\
Mistral-7B & 0 & 3.22   & 2.90  & 0.008 & 0.192 \\
Mistral-7B & 1 & 1.64   & 3.72  & 0.003 & 0.160 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Three regularities hold in all nine pairs: SLEEP's BCP always follows a step-then-plateau
trajectory (early cycles protected, then a discrete jump to a level that holds flat to cycle ten);
naive LoRA's recall is higher in eight of nine pairs; and no run of either arm keeps
$\mathrm{BCP}<1.05$ past cycle two. What does \emph{not} replicate is the plateau level --- $2.7$ to
$10.2$ across three seeds of the identical Qwen-7B configuration, $4.8$ versus $1{,}531$ on Llama
--- three orders of magnitude on unchanged hyperparameters, flipping the cycle-ten ``winner'' seed
to seed (SLEEP 4/9, naive 5/9).

The claim we now make is methodological and, we believe, stronger than the one it replaces:
\textbf{fixed-horizon, single-run comparisons of continual-learning architectures are unreliable at
the horizons and seed counts this literature typically reports.} The clipping mechanism guarantees a
damage ceiling exists; where it lands is effectively a lottery over the optimisation path. We
recommend such comparisons state a horizon sweep and a seed spread, or not be made.

% ===== 4. The extension ====================================================
\section{The warm-up: a replicated null}

The pre-registered criterion ($\mathrm{DRA}\;0.10$--$0.15$ at $\mathrm{BCP}<1.5$) was met nowhere.
Across sixteen runs --- two variants, two seeds, four models --- recall moved by at most
$\pm 0.008$. The 36-parameter gate converges to identity on every model. The LoRA variant
demonstrably learns memory-conditioned prediction everywhere (loss falls $\sim$1 nat) and lifts
recognition where the model survives, yet recall never follows; its preservation cost is
family-dependent and, on Llama and Mistral at the untuned learning rate, catastrophic
($\mathrm{BCP}\;10^{4}$--$10^{6}$). One instructive side-finding: on Mistral the gate learned to
\emph{suppress} memory interference it could not learn to exploit, reducing the cost of carrying a
populated bank from $8.9\times$ to $2.8\times$.

The diagnosis is thereby strengthened twice over: the recognition--recall gap survived a principled,
precedent-grounded repair attempt, on every family, and the repair moved recognition and recall
\emph{differentially} --- an experimental reproduction of the dissociation itself.

in-context is retrieval failure, not comprehension failure.

% ===== 6. Paper state ======================================================
\section{The paper as it now stands}

All results above are integrated into the revised paper as primary content --- cross-model tables in
the results, the nine-run horizon/seed analysis replacing the earlier multi-cycle section, the
sixteen-run warm-up table, and the verification-gate methodology in the annexure. The title is
unchanged. The contribution set, compressed: a universal recognition--recall dissociation with an
in-context control; a universal, near-total proxy-validation failure; a replicated null on the
lightweight repair; the precision-floor amendment separated into dtype and magnitude failures; and
the long-horizon instability result. The paper no longer contains any headline claim resting on a
single model or a single seed.

% ===== 7. Recommendation ===================================================
\section{Recommendation and requested input}

We consider the empirical programme closed at this scale. The remaining open items are genuinely
next-paper material rather than gaps: a many-seed characterisation of the plateau distribution, the
warm-up at Memorizing-Transformers training scale with per-family learning rates, and extension to
mixture-of-experts and larger models.

We would welcome your judgement on two points: whether the horizon-and-seed instability result is
best kept inside this paper (our view: yes, as its methodological anchor) or split into a short
companion note aimed at the continual-learning evaluation literature; and the target venue and
deadline, so the submission draft can be formatted accordingly.
