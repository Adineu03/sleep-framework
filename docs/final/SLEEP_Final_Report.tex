% ===== Preamble ============================================================
Professor Guha,

This report replaces the 8~August final and tells the programme's story whole, in the order the
evidence arrived: the system as originally built, the failure it exhibited, the replication
programme that made that failure rigorous, the re-examination that located its causes, and the
validation matrix that turned it into a working, baseline-controlled result. The one-line arc:
\textbf{a recall floor of $0.006$ that survived every within-paradigm repair rose to $0.750$ once we
moved the consolidation write to where the transformer actually stores facts} --- and the repaired
system now beats both naive fine-tuning and an EWC baseline in its design regime, under
pre-registered gates. Every number below traces to a released run file.

% ===== 1 ====================================================================
\section{What SLEEP was}

By late April, SLEEP was a complete, pre-registered implementation of biological memory
consolidation in a transformer: a tagging layer that flags surprising spans by prediction error, a
direct-write KV memory bank, a PRP-style resource allocator, and a sleep engine that replays
selected memories into a LoRA consolidation adapter under four safety mechanisms (EWC, hard
clipping, interleaving, validation rollback). Thirty-six design questions were resolved in a
formalization document before any code was written.

The first campaign (Qwen2.5-7B, 200 synthetic single-exposure facts) produced the observation that
named the paper: injected memories bias four-way \emph{recognition} by $+0.16$ over untagged facts,
yet \emph{free-form recall} of the same facts is $0.00$ --- and no safety setting in a four-point
sweep achieved useful recall at acceptable damage. Around it sat two more single-run observations:
the internal validator certified consolidations that failed any direct test, and a three-cycle
comparison suggested SLEEP preserved capability better than naive fine-tuning. Everything rested on
one model and one seed.

% ===== 2 ====================================================================
\section{What the review named}

Your review of that first evidence, together with the external review, converged on one demand in
eleven items: \emph{nothing is replicated, nothing is compared properly, and the repair the
diagnosis implies has not been tried.} Concretely: multiple seeds, extended horizons (10+ cycles),
more models and precisions, real baselines (RAG, EWC-only, in-context), an external validation
gate, tightened biological framing, and the retrieval-aware fine-tune.

% ===== 3 ====================================================================
\section{Hostile replication: what we did, what it found}

We rebuilt the programme around those demands --- five seeds per result, four models across three
families (Qwen2.5-7B/1.5B, Llama-3.1-8B, Mistral-7B), ten-cycle horizons, all baselines, a
two-stage validation gate, and a ground-truth verification harness that checks the injection path
on real hardware before any run (it rejected two defective configurations; no invalid number ever
entered the record). The findings:

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}l c c c c@{}}
\toprule
 & Qwen-7B & Qwen-1.5B & Llama-8B & Mistral-7B \\
\midrule
Recall from consolidated weights (5 seeds) & $0.006$ & $0.007$ & $0.006$ & $0.007$ \\
Recall with the fact in context            & $0.760$ & $0.700$ & $0.987$ & $0.927$ \\
Proxy validation false-confirmation        & $\sim$97\% & 100\% & $\sim$93\% & 100\% \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The failure was universal: every model reads and uses a fact it can see, and recalls essentially
nothing consolidated into its weights; internal surprise-based validation is wrong about
$93$--$100\%$ of the consolidations it certifies, everywhere. The earlier three-cycle preservation
claim failed replication --- across nine seeded ten-cycle run-pairs the clip-induced damage plateau
varies by three orders of magnitude on identical hyperparameters ($2.7 \to 1{,}531$), so we
withdrew that claim and replaced it with the stronger methodological finding that fixed-horizon,
single-run continual-learning comparisons are unreliable. Most uncomfortably: \textbf{EWC alone
(DRA $0.070$ at BCP $1.12$) beat every full-pipeline configuration on recall-per-damage.}

% ===== 4 ====================================================================
\section{What we added beyond the checklist}

Two self-initiated additions defined this phase. The \textbf{retrieval-aware warm-up} --- the
repair the recognition--recall diagnosis implies --- was promoted from side-item to a
pre-registered extension (success bar fixed in advance: DRA $0.10$--$0.15$ at BCP $<1.5$) and
tested properly: two variants, two seeds, four models. It is a replicated null: across sixteen runs
recall moved by at most $\pm 0.008$, while recognition improved where the model survived --- the
dissociation reproduced under intervention. And the \textbf{plateau lottery} --- the
three-orders-of-magnitude seed variance above --- emerged only because horizon and seed demands
were run \emph{together}; it generalises past SLEEP to how the continual-learning literature
reports results.

At this point the paper was an honest, universal, well-controlled diagnosis --- of a failure. Its
title said ``Empirical Limits.'' The question we could not close: is consolidation into frozen
LLMs impossible, or were we doing it wrong?

% ===== 5 ====================================================================
\section{The re-examination: three stacked causes}

Before accepting the negative result, we ran a pre-mortem on the paper: assume the conclusion is
wrong --- where would the error be? Re-deriving the design from first principles against the
knowledge-editing literature (Geva et~al.; ROME; MEMIT) exposed an assumption that had never been
questioned: \textbf{the consolidation adapter's placement was chosen by biological analogy}
(attention projections, top third of layers, playing ``neocortex''), while causal-tracing evidence
locates factual associations in \emph{mid-stack MLP} modules. Two further suspects followed from
the same pass: single-wording training (cloze succeeded while recall failed --- the signature of
string memorization rather than extractable knowledge) and sampled decoding in
knowledge-presence evaluation (replaced with greedy throughout).

A five-arm isolation ladder (50 facts, matched exposure, two models, two seeds) tested the
hypothesis:

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}l c c@{}}
\toprule
Arm (Qwen2.5-7B) & Free-form recall & BCP \\
\midrule
attention-top, single wording (original) & $0.01$ & $3.6$ \\
mid-MLP, single wording                  & $0.03$ & $1.2$--$1.8$ \\
mid-MLP $+$ paraphrases                  & $0.22$ & $1.4$ \\
mid-MLP $+$ paraphrases $+$ distillation & $\mathbf{0.36}$ & $\mathbf{0.83}$ \\
attention-top $+$ distillation (control) & $0.06$ & $2.9$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Placement alone halves damage in 8 of 8 comparisons; paraphrase diversity moves cloze \emph{down}
and recall \emph{up} (memorization becoming extraction); distillation from an in-context teacher is
the strongest and gentlest write; and the control shows the objective cannot rescue the wrong
substrate. Integration then revealed a \textbf{third stacked cause}: the safety machinery as
designed ($\delta_{\max}=0.01$) erased the write inside the full pipeline (trained-subset DRA
$0.007$, three seeds), while a moderate calibration ($\delta_{\max}=0.1$) preserved both recall and
protection ($0.189 \pm 0.031$ at BCP $0.882 \pm 0.027$, five seeds, every seed under $1.0$). Each
cause alone suffices to hold recall at the floor --- which is why no single-factor intervention in
the diagnosis era ever moved it.

% ===== 6 ====================================================================
\section{The validated system: the four-model matrix}

The full validation ran 11--12 August as a staged campaign whose gates, predictions, and stop-loss
were committed to the repository before execution: 35 runs, 19.7 GPU-hours, $\approx$USD~\$15.5,
zero failures. Every gate was read as written, including the one Llama fails.

\subsection{Single cycle, five seeds per model (trained-subset DRA @ BCP)}

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}l l c c@{}}
\toprule
Model & Recipe & DRA & BCP \\
\midrule
Mistral-7B   & distil, $\delta_{\max}{=}0.1$   & $\mathbf{0.750 \pm 0.033}$ & $1.21 \pm 0.06$ \\
Llama-3.1-8B & par.-CE, $\delta_{\max}{=}0.03$ & $0.512 \pm 0.048$ & $2.09 \pm 0.14$ \\
Qwen2.5-7B   & distil, $\delta_{\max}{=}0.1$   & $0.189 \pm 0.031$ & $0.88 \pm 0.03$ \\
Qwen2.5-1.5B & distil, $\delta_{\max}{=}0.1$   & $0.105 \pm 0.020$ & $1.04 \pm 0.02$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Every model sits $17$--$125\times$ above the old floor; the strongest result is on the family the
recipe never touched during development, which argues against overfitting. Llama is the flagged
exception: a pre-registered clip ladder ($0.1 \to 0.05 \to 0.03$) reduced damage monotonically
($4.35 \to 2.14$) without reaching the $1.5$ bar --- its bleed is not clip-dominated, and it is
reported as \emph{not yet tuned}, not as solved.

Three seed-consistent findings. \textbf{The design-intent claim is now real}: SLEEP exceeds naive
fine-tuning on cumulative recall on all four models, both seeds ($3\times$ on Qwen-7B; on Mistral
it wins both axes at once). \textbf{The machinery is load-bearing at small scale}: naive training
detonates the 1.5B model (BCP $24.5$/$11.4$, seed-random size --- the plateau lottery reproduced)
while SLEEP holds $\approx 2.4$. \textbf{The EWC dissociation}: the matched EWC arm (identical
adapter, steps, learning rate; only the penalty differs) posts the best damage numbers with recall
at naive's level --- regularisation prevents forgetting; it does not create extraction. SLEEP's
across-seed spread is $\approx 0.005$ everywhere; the seed lottery of Section~3 belongs to
\emph{unconstrained} arms. On Llama, EWC-only is competitive with the not-yet-tuned SLEEP ---
reported plainly.

% ===== 7 ====================================================================
\section{Where SLEEP stands, and what we ask}

SLEEP set out as a memory system, became a rigorous diagnosis of why the obvious version fails, and
is now a working consolidation system with receipts: a frozen model reads once, sleeps, and answers
questions from its own weights --- validated across three families and two scales, against real
baselines, under pre-registered gates. The diagnostic findings stand on their own (the
recognition--recall dissociation for injected memory, the near-total proxy-validation failure, the
seed-instability of unconstrained baselines), and the meta-lesson generalises: \textbf{biological
mechanisms transfer; biological anatomy does not} --- the substrate must come from the
transformer's own causal structure. Named gaps, carried into future work rather than hidden:
cumulative recall decays as early batches fade (interleaved rehearsal --- what biological sleep
actually does --- is the clearest next mechanism); damage leaks $\approx +0.12$/cycle in every arm;
Llama requires per-family learning-rate work.

The paper has been restructured around this complete arc (31 pages, two movements, nine figures,
retitled \emph{``Recognition Without Recall: Diagnosing and Repairing Biologically-Inspired Memory
Consolidation in Transformers''}) and is ready for review. We would welcome your judgement on:
(i) whether the diagnosis-era first half is correctly weighted against the repair; (ii) target
venue and deadline; and (iii) whether the interleaved-rehearsal follow-up should be scoped now or
after review.
